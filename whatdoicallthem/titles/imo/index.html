이모 imo

이모 is a precise kinship term that has acquired a second, entirely different life in restaurants. Both uses are current, and mixing them up is harmless — but knowing the difference tells you a lot about how Korean family words work.

Hangul이모
Revised Romanizationimo
McCune–Reischauerimo
Literallymother's sister
Said byany speaker
Said toa maternal aunt — and, affectionately, restaurant staff

The kinship meaning

이모 is your mother's sister. Korean does not have a single word for "aunt": the mother's side and the father's side get different vocabulary, and the mother's sister is 이모 while the father's sister is 고모 (gomo). The distinction is not optional or old-fashioned — it is how the relationships are named.

The same split runs through the whole system. Maternal grandparents are 외할머니 and 외할아버지, with the 외- prefix marking the mother's side; the mother's brother is 외삼촌. A learner who grasps that one prefix has understood a large part of the Korean kinship map.

The restaurant meaning

In small, family-run Korean restaurants, customers commonly address the woman working there as 이모 or 이모님. It is affectionate rather than literal — the point is that she is being treated like family rather than like staff, and it is warmer than 아주머니 and much warmer than 아줌마.

It is a register, not a rule. It fits a neighbourhood 백반집 or a pojangmacha; it does not fit a hotel restaurant, where 저기요 followed by a proper title is correct.

Related terms worth knowing

이모부 is your 이모's husband. 고모 is the father's sister, and 고모부 her husband. 외숙모 is the wife of your mother's brother. These compounds are built systematically, which is why a Korean speaker can name a relative four steps away without hesitating.

Children also use 이모 for their mother's close female friends, in the same borrowed-family spirit as the restaurant use.

The usual mistake — Using 이모 for any aunt. The father's sister is 고모, and getting them the wrong way round is a genuine error rather than a stylistic slip.

Example sentences

이모, 오랜만이에요.
Imo, oraenmanieyo.
Auntie, it's been a while.
이모님, 여기 반찬 좀 더 주세요.
Imo-nim, yeogi banchan jom deo juseyo.
Excuse me, could we have more side dishes?
이모가 김치를 보내 주셨어요.
Imo-ga gimchireul bonae jusyeosseoyo.
My aunt sent us some kimchi.
우리 이모는 대구에 사세요.
Uri imo-neun Daegue saseyo.
My aunt lives in Daegu.

Frequently asked questions

Q. What is the difference between imo and gomo?
이모 is your mother's sister; 고모 is your father's sister. Korean names the two sides separately.
Q. Can I call a restaurant worker imo?
In a small, casual, family-run place, yes — 이모 or 이모님 is friendly and common. In a formal restaurant use 저기요 and then a proper title.
Q. What about my aunt's husband?
이모부 (imobu) for your mother's sister's husband; 고모부 for your father's sister's husband.
