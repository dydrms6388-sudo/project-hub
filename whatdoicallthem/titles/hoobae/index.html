후배 hubae

후배 is the mirror of 선배, and the single most important fact about it is negative: unlike almost every other word on this list, you do not use it to someone's face.

Hangul후배
Revised Romanizationhubae
McCune–Reischauerhubae
Literallysomeone who came after
Said byany speaker
Said tonobody — it is a description, not a form of address

What it actually means

후배 is anyone who entered your school, club, department or company after you did. The relationship is exactly the inverse of 선배, and the same boundary applies — it is defined by a shared institution, not by age, and it lapses once you are both outside that institution.

You will hear it constantly in third person. 우리 후배예요 ("she's my junior") introduces someone; 후배들이랑 밥 먹었어 ("I ate with the juniors") describes an evening. In all of these the word is doing descriptive work.

Why you never call someone 후배

Korean address terms that point downward are essentially absent. 선배 elevates the listener, which is why it can be spoken to them; 후배 would announce that you outrank them, and saying that out loud is what makes it land badly. There is no polite version — adding -님 does not rescue it.

What you actually say to a junior is their given name, with 아 after a consonant and 야 after a vowel if you are close (민준아, 지우야), or their name plus 씨 in a workplace or with someone you do not know well. In a company, a junior with any position is still addressed by that position plus -님.

The obligations that come with having 후배

Being the senior in the pair is not only a privilege. The expectation is that you make time for questions, hand over materials without being asked twice, and — in the traditional version of the script — pay when the group eats. Korean juniors notice quickly which seniors do this and which do not.

For a learner joining a Korean club or workplace, the useful move is to identify who your 후배 are, and then never use the word to them.

The usual mistake — Calling a junior 후배 to their face, in the belief that it mirrors 선배. It does not. Use their name.

Example sentences

이분은 제 후배예요.
Ibuneun je hubaeyeyo.
This is my junior (from the same school).
후배들이랑 저녁 먹었어요.
Hubaedeurirang jeonyeok meogeosseoyo.
I had dinner with the juniors.
지우야, 이거 좀 봐 줄래?
Jiu-ya, igeo jom bwa jullae?
Jiu, could you take a look at this? (what you say instead)
민준 씨, 이 부분 확인 부탁해요.
Minjun-ssi, i bubun hwagin butakaeyo.
Minjun-ssi, please check this part. (at work)

Frequently asked questions

Q. Can I say hoobae-nim?
No. The -님 does not fix it, because the problem is the direction of the word rather than its politeness level.
Q. So what do I call a junior?
Their given name plus 아 or 야 if you are close, their name plus 씨 if you are not, or their job title plus -님 at work.
Q. Is hoobae rude?
Not as a description. 제 후배예요 is entirely neutral. It becomes a problem only as a form of address.
