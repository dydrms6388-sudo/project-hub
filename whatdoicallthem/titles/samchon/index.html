삼촌 samchon

삼촌 is the ordinary word for an uncle on the father's side, and it is also a window into the arithmetic that sits underneath the whole Korean kinship system.

Hangul삼촌
Revised Romanizationsamchon
McCune–Reischauersamch'on
Literallyfather's brother, literally 'three degrees'
Said byany speaker
Said toan uncle on the father's side

The number in the word

촌 (chon) is a unit of kinship distance. Each parent–child step counts as one 촌. You to your father is 1촌; your father to his own father is another, and back down to your father's brother is a third — so an uncle is 삼촌, three degrees. Your sibling is 2촌, a first cousin is 4촌, and Korean speakers still routinely say 사촌 (sachon) for cousin without thinking about the arithmetic.

The system is why Korean can specify a relationship precisely with a single number, and why the phrase 몇 촌이에요? ("how many degrees?") is a real question at a family gathering.

Which uncle, exactly

삼촌 in everyday speech is the father's brother — most often an unmarried one. Once he marries, the more formal term 작은아버지 (jageun-abeoji, "younger father") is used for a father's younger brother, and 큰아버지 (keun-abeoji, "older father") for a father's older brother. Their wives are 작은어머니 and 큰어머니.

The mother's brother is a different word again: 외삼촌 (oesamchon), with the 외- prefix that marks the mother's side, and his wife is 외숙모 (oesukmo). Many Korean families use plain 삼촌 loosely for all of these in casual speech, but the precise terms are what appear at weddings, funerals and on official forms.

The borrowed use

As with 이모, children extend 삼촌 to their parents' close male friends, and the man in question is expected to behave accordingly — presents at holidays, pocket money, an ally in family arguments. It is a small piece of how Korean social life borrows family vocabulary for people who are not family.

For a learner, the honest position is that 삼촌 and 이모 are the two kinship words most likely to be used at you before you are related to anyone.

The usual mistake — Assuming 삼촌 covers every uncle. The mother's brother is 외삼촌, and a married paternal uncle is normally 큰아버지 or 작은아버지.

Example sentences

Hello, uncle.

My uncle bought me a bicycle.
외삼촌은 부산에 계세요.

My uncle (mother's brother) lives in Busan.
우리는 사촌이에요.
Urineun sachonieyo.
We are cousins.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Why does samchon contain the number three?
촌 counts parent–child steps. An uncle is three steps away from you, hence 삼촌. A cousin is four, hence 사촌.
Q. What is the mother's brother called?
외삼촌 (oesamchon). The 외- prefix marks the mother's side throughout Korean kinship vocabulary.
Q. Does samchon change when he marries?
In precise usage yes — 큰아버지 for a father's older brother and 작은아버지 for a younger one. Casual speech often keeps 삼촌.
