형 hyeong

형 is the male-to-male half of the grid, and among Korean men it does something the English word "mate" only half does: switching to 형 is the visible moment a relationship stops being formal.

Hangul형
Revised Romanizationhyeong
McCune–Reischauerhyŏng
Literallya man's older brother
Said bymale speakers only
Said toan older male you are close to

What it actually means

형 is a male speaker's elder brother, extended to older friends, teammates, seniors and anyone else a man has become comfortable with. In a family it is compulsory. Outside it, the moment two men agree to drop formal speech, the younger one usually starts saying 형 in the same conversation — the two changes travel together.

The word is short, and because it ends in ㅇ it takes particles cleanly: 형이, 형한테, 형이랑. Learners often over-lengthen it into "hyung-ah"; the vocative suffix is not used here, and 형 stands alone.

Why the spelling looks odd

Revised Romanization gives hyeong, because ㅕ is yeo. The spelling hyung that dominates English-language writing about Korea reflects the sound as English ears parse it, and it has become the de facto standard outside academic work. McCune–Reischauer writes hyŏng with a breve.

In every case the Korean is the same single syllable. If you are learning to read Hangul, 형 is a useful early word precisely because the romanizations disagree and the Hangul does not.

Where it stops working

The workplace. A Korean office runs on position plus -님 — 팀장님, 과장님, 부장님 — and that outranks age completely. Calling a senior colleague 형 in front of other people puts him in an awkward spot, because it implies a private closeness the room has not agreed to. Wait for him to offer it, and even then keep the job title in meetings.

The other limit is distance. 형 claims friendship. With an older man you have met twice, 이름 + 씨 or 선배님 is the correct holding position, and nobody will read it as cold.

The usual mistake — Using 형 upward at work, and female speakers using it at all. A woman in the same position says 오빠 (oppa).

Frequently asked questions

Q. Can a woman say hyung?
No. A female speaker addressing an older male says 오빠 (oppa). You will occasionally hear women use 형 as deliberate slang inside very casual groups, but it is a marked choice, not the rule.
Q. Is it hyung or hyeong?
hyeong is Revised Romanization; hyung is the informal spelling used almost everywhere in English. Same word, 형.
Q. Can I call my boss hyung?
Not by default. Use his position plus -님. If he explicitly invites 형 outside work, use it there and keep the title in the office.
