아저씨 ajeossi

아저씨 is a plain descriptive noun that has picked up an edge. It is the word for a middle-aged man, and it is also, for many men, an unwelcome announcement that they have become one.

Hangul아저씨
Revised Romanizationajeossi
McCune–Reischauerajŏssi
Literallya middle-aged man
Said byany speaker
Said toa middle-aged male stranger — with care

What it actually means

In the dictionary sense 아저씨 covers an adult man from roughly his late thirties upward who is not a relative and whose name and position you do not know. Historically it also named a specific kinship slot — a father's cousin, roughly — but the general use has long since displaced that.

It is used most naturally about a role: 택배 아저씨 (the delivery man), 경비 아저씨 (the building's security guard). In that framing it is warm and completely ordinary, and Korean children use it constantly.

Why it stings anyway

As a direct address, 아저씨 makes an unrequested statement about age. Plenty of men in their thirties bristle at it for exactly that reason, and being called 아저씨 by someone in their twenties is a small, recognisable joke in Korean about crossing an invisible line.

It is not an insult in the way 아줌마 can be, and it is not offensive in general use. But it is blunt, and there is a better option in almost every situation.

What to say instead

To get a stranger's attention, use 저기요 (jeogiyo) — "excuse me". It assumes nothing about age or gender and works everywhere from a train platform to a restaurant.

Once you are talking, upgrade to a title that names their role rather than their age: 기사님 for a driver, 사장님 for a shop owner or restaurateur, 선생님 as a generic respectful form for an adult. Any of these will be received better than 아저씨.

The usual mistake — Using 아저씨 as a default address for any adult man. It works about a role and it is risky to a face — 저기요 first, then a role title.

Example sentences

저기요, 이거 떨어뜨리셨어요.
Jeogiyo, igeo tteoreotteurisyeosseoyo.
Excuse me, you dropped this. (the safe version)
기사님, 여기서 내려 주세요.
Gisa-nim, yeogiseo naeryeo juseyo.
Driver, please let me off here.
택배 아저씨가 방금 다녀가셨어요.
Taekbae ajeossi-ga banggeum danyeogasyeosseoyo.
The delivery man just came by.
사장님, 이거 얼마예요?
Sajang-nim, igeo eolmayeyo?
Excuse me (shop owner), how much is this?

Frequently asked questions

Q. Is ajusshi offensive?
Not inherently — it is a plain noun. But as direct address it comments on someone's age, and many men would rather you used 저기요 or a role title.
Q. How is it spelled properly?
Revised Romanization gives ajeossi. The spellings ajusshi and ahjussi come from subtitles and fan writing.
Q. What is the female equivalent?
아주머니 / 아줌마 — and that one is considerably more sensitive.
