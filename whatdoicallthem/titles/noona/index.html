누나 nuna

누나 completes the four-term grid: male speaker, older female listener. It behaves much like 형, with one extra thing to watch — it puts an age difference into the open, and not everyone wants that.

Hangul누나
Revised Romanizationnuna
McCune–Reischauernuna
Literallya man's older sister
Said bymale speakers only
Said toan older female you are close to

What it actually means

누나 is a male speaker's elder sister, extended outward to older friends, seniors, colleagues and girlfriends. Inside the family it is the only option. Outside it, a man switches to 누나 at roughly the same moment he would switch to 형 with an older man: when the relationship becomes comfortable and the formal speech drops.

It also works in third person. 누나가 추천해 줬어 means "she recommended it", with the referent understood from context. Like the other three kinship terms, it is a relationship label first and an address term second.

The sensitivity

Because 누나 states out loud that the woman is older, some women dislike hearing it from a man they have just met — it foregrounds an age gap that a neutral form would leave alone. The reliable approach is the same as everywhere else in Korean: start on 이름 + 씨, and move to 누나 once you are genuinely friendly, or once she uses casual speech with you.

In a dating context the calculus flips. A man dating an older woman calling her 누나 is completely ordinary, and many couples keep it permanently. What matters is that the closeness came first.

At work

The office rule does not bend for 누나 either. A senior colleague is her job title plus -님, however friendly you are, and especially in front of other people. Kinship terms belong to the after-hours version of the same relationship.

One useful exception: in the arts, in start-ups and in small creative teams that have flattened their titles, 누나 and 형 do circulate at work. Watch what the room does before joining in.

The usual mistake — Female speakers using 누나 — they say 언니 (eonni) — and men reaching for it with a woman they barely know, where it can read as a comment on her age.

Example sentences

누나, 이거 좀 도와줄 수 있어?
Nuna, igeo jom dowajul su isseo?
Noona, can you help me with this?
누나가 추천해 준 영화야.
Nuna-ga chucheonhae jun yeonghwaya.
It's the film she recommended.
누나 먼저 드세요.
Nuna meonjeo deuseyo.
You go ahead and eat first.
우리 누나는 부산에 살아요.
Uri nuna-neun Busane sarayo.
My older sister lives in Busan.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Can a woman say noona?
No. A female speaker addressing an older female says 언니 (eonni).
Q. Is noona rude?
Not in itself. It is affectionate. It can land badly only when used too early, because it names the age gap explicitly.
Q. Is it noona or nuna?
nuna is Revised Romanization; noona is the common informal spelling. Both are 누나.
