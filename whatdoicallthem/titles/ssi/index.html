씨 -ssi

If you remember only one form from this whole site, make it 이름 + 씨. It is the setting Korean adults default to with each other before closeness or rank tells them what else to use.

Hangul씨
Revised Romanization-ssi
McCune–Reischauer-ssi
Literallyan honorific suffix attached to a name
Said byany speaker
Said toa polite equal, or an adult you do not know well

What it actually means

씨 is an honorific suffix, roughly the weight of "Mr" or "Ms" but attached to a name rather than standing in front of it: 지우 씨, 민준 씨, 김지우 씨. It marks polite distance between adults who are broadly equal, and it makes no claim about age, gender or relationship, which is exactly why it works so well as a first move.

It is the standard address for a new colleague at your own level, a classmate on a university course, someone you have been introduced to at a gathering, or anyone whose position you do not yet know.

Given name, not family name

Attach 씨 to the given name (지우 씨) or the full name (김지우 씨). Attaching it to the family name alone — 김 씨 — is the one arrangement to avoid: it sounds curt and slightly dismissive, and in some contexts it is how you would refer to a stranger whose name you happened to overhear.

Korean names put the family name first, so "Kim Jiu" means the given name is Jiu. 지우 씨 is the everyday form; 김지우 씨 is the formal one used in meetings, emails and announcements.

The blind spot

씨 does not travel upward. Using it with someone clearly senior to you — your boss, a much older person, a professor — sounds presumptuous, because it treats them as an equal. For those people you need the position plus -님 (팀장님, 교수님) or 선생님.

It also cannot be used with your own family, and it sounds strange with a close friend, where it reads as a deliberate pulling-back. Between those two boundaries — not a superior, not an intimate — it is the safest word in Korean.

The usual mistake — Family name plus 씨 (김 씨), and using 씨 with a boss or a professor. The first sounds brusque; the second sounds like you have not registered the hierarchy.

Example sentences

지우 씨, 이 부분 확인 부탁드려요.
Jiu-ssi, i bubun hwagin butakdeuryeoyo.
Jiu-ssi, could you check this part?
민준 씨는 어떻게 생각하세요?
Minjun-ssi-neun eotteoke saenggakhaseyo?
What do you think, Minjun-ssi?
김지우 씨, 회의실로 와 주세요.
Gim Jiu-ssi, hoeuisillo wa juseyo.
Ms Kim Jiu, please come to the meeting room.
지우 씨, 점심 같이 하실래요?
Jiu-ssi, jeomsim gachi hasillaeyo?
Jiu-ssi, shall we have lunch together?

Frequently asked questions

Q. Is -ssi like Mr or Ms?
Close, but it goes on the given name and it does not scale upward. English "Mr" works for a superior; 씨 does not.
Q. Can I use -ssi with my boss?
No. Use their position plus -님, such as 팀장님 or 부장님.
Q. Is it written with a space?
Standard orthography puts a space before 씨 when it follows a name: 지우 씨. You will see it written closed up informally.
