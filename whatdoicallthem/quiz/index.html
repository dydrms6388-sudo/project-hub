What this quiz is testing

Korean address terms are one of the few parts of the language where a learner can be grammatically perfect and still land badly. The verb endings are teachable in a week; knowing that a 32-year-old team leader outranks a 45-year-old subordinate, or that 후배 is a word you use about someone and never to them, takes longer because it is social knowledge rather than grammar. These fifteen questions cover the decisions that come up most: the four kinship terms and the speaker-gender rule behind them, the entry-order pair 선배 and 후배, the two suffixes -씨 and -님, what happens in an office, what to say to a stranger, and the age arithmetic that sits underneath all of it.

Nothing here is trick knowledge. Every answer is explained the moment you choose, so a wrong guess still teaches you the rule, and the summary at the end lists only the ones you got wrong so you know where to read next. If you want the underlying reference rather than the test, each term has its own page in the title reference, and the honorifics guide covers the speech levels the quiz assumes.

Frequently asked questions

Q. How is the quiz scored?
Fifteen questions, one point each. You find out immediately whether you were right, with the reason, and the running score sits at the top. At the end you get a grade band and a card you can save.
Q. Do I need to read Hangul?
No. Every Korean word appears in Hangul with Revised Romanization beside it, and the questions are in English throughout.
Q. Is my score stored anywhere?
No. Everything runs in your browser. A challenge link carries the score inside the address after ?r=, not on a server.
