Korean age: three systems, one 2023 law

Until recently a Korean could truthfully give three different answers to “how old are you?”. The law has settled the question on paper; the habits are taking longer.

The three systems

세는 나이 (se-neun nai), the traditional Korean age: you are 1 at birth and everyone gains a year on 1 January. A baby born on 31 December is 2 years old the next morning. This is the system that produced the famous "Korean age is one or two years higher" rule of thumb.

만 나이 (man nai), international age: 0 at birth, +1 on your birthday. Identical to how age works in English-speaking countries.

연 나이 (yeon nai), calendar-year age: current year minus birth year, ignoring the birthday. This one has never been about tradition — it is an administrative convenience, and it still governs school entry, military conscription and the legal age for alcohol and tobacco.

What the 2023 law actually did

On 28 June 2023 an amendment to Korea's framework legislation made 만 나이 the default for all laws and contracts, unless a specific statute says otherwise. In practice this removed a long-running ambiguity in insurance policies, medical dosing, employment terms and pension eligibility, where the same word had meant different numbers to different parties.

What it did not do is abolish 연 나이. School years, conscription and the drinking age still run on the calendar-year calculation, which is why a Korean 18-year-old born in December can legally buy alcohol before an 18-year-old born the following March.

It also did not change how people talk. 세는 나이 survives in everyday conversation, in the way parents describe small children, and — crucially for anyone learning the language — in the seniority reckoning that decides whether someone is your 형 or your 친구. That reckoning was always about birth year rather than age, so the law left it untouched.

How to work out which one someone means

Since 2023 the safe default is international age, especially in writing, at a hospital or on any form. In conversation, if the number sounds a year or two high for the person in front of you, they are probably giving you 세는 나이 out of habit. The clean way to resolve it without an awkward exchange is to ask for the birth year instead: 몇 년생이세요? Everyone knows their birth year, and the birth year is what the social hierarchy actually uses.

Quick conversion

  • Korean age → international age: subtract 1 if your birthday has already passed this year, subtract 2 if it has not.
  • International age → Korean age: add 1 if your birthday has passed, add 2 if it has not.
  • Calendar-year age: current year minus birth year, always.

Worked example for someone born in October 1996, reckoned in March 2026: international age 29 (birthday not yet reached), calendar-year age 30, traditional Korean age 31.

Why this still matters for address terms

Because the hierarchy runs on birth year, none of this changes who calls whom 형. Two people born in the same year are 동갑 and equals regardless of which system either of them quotes. Someone born the year before you is senior to you even if, on a given day in international age, you are momentarily the same number. If you remember one thing, make it that: for choosing a title, compare birth years, not ages.

Frequently asked questions

Q. Did Korea abolish Korean age?
It made international age (만 나이) the legal default from 28 June 2023. Traditional 세는 나이 still appears in everyday speech, and calendar-year age still governs school entry, conscription and the drinking age.
Q. So how old is a Korean person really?
Since 2023, assume international age unless context suggests otherwise. If the number seems a year or two high, they are quoting the traditional count.
Q. Why does everyone ask for the birth year instead?
Because the birth year is unambiguous, and because seniority — which decides the titles and speech level — has always been calculated from it rather than from any age number.
Q. Can I still buy alcohol on my international 19th birthday?
The drinking age uses calendar-year age: you may buy alcohol from 1 January of the year you turn 19, whatever your birthday.
Q. Does the change affect what I call someone?
No. Address terms follow birth year, and the 2023 law did not touch that.
